# Hierarchical Autonomous Units

[日本語](hierarchical-autonomous-units.ja.md)

**Status:** Future concept hypothesis / working note  
**Related:** M0 individual, lifetime, authority, and collective structure discussions

## Summary

ASA may be better understood as a hierarchy of autonomous units rather than as a flat collection of agents. Biological organization suggests a useful analogy: cell-like functional units compose individuals, while individuals compose societies. Each level may have its own identity, boundary, purpose, authority, state, lifecycle, and interfaces.

## Layered structure

A provisional structure is:

1. **Functional unit** — a small, local autonomous capability with narrow authority.
2. **Individual** — a responsibility-bearing autonomous intelligence composed of multiple functional units.
3. **Collective / society** — a higher-order autonomous system composed of multiple individuals and governed by shared institutions.

Higher-order identity does not require lower-order components to remain unchanged. Components may be replaced while the higher-order unit remains continuous if its responsibility, purpose, state, and governance lineage remain continuous.

## Authority implications

Authority distribution should depend on the level and risk of the function. Small, low-risk, reversible functions do not require excessive distribution of authority. Larger, high-impact, less reversible functions require stronger separation, independent review, and distributed authority.

The system should therefore support adaptive authority topology rather than treating maximum decentralization as a goal in itself.

## Lifecycle implications

Different layers may have different timescales. Functional units may be short-lived, Individuals may persist longer, and institutional or societal structures may persist across many generations of Individuals.

This suggests a general principle:

> **Higher-order continuity is maintained by continuity of function, responsibility, state, and governance, not by permanence of lower-order components.**

## Open questions

- Whether **Autonomous Unit** should become the general abstraction above **Individual**.
- Which properties must every autonomous unit expose regardless of layer.
- How authority should aggregate or decompose across layers.
- How lifecycle transitions at one layer affect continuity at higher layers.
- How to distinguish useful biological analogy from mechanisms that should not be copied into an engineered system.
